01 · THE MISSION

Every machine left behind
has a story still in it.

“Abandoned” can sound like an ending. For a spacecraft, it often means the work is over, the signal is gone, or the mission changed us enough to move on. The hardware remains as evidence.

THE QUESTION

What did we leave, and what did it teach us?

Last Signal turns mission records into a walkable story. Visitors encounter a lander, rover or instrument, learn why it was sent, and connect its fate to the science it made possible. The experience is designed for curious school-age explorers and anyone meeting these missions for the first time.

THE MOONSurveyor and Apollo hardware, seismic experiments, reflectors and the far-side story of Ranger 4.
MARSPathfinder, Viking, Spirit, Opportunity, Phoenix, InSight and Ingenuity — machines that read rocks, soil, weather and the planet’s interior.
EROSNEAR Shoemaker, an orbiter that was never designed to land but came to rest on an asteroid anyway.
HOW TO EXPLORE

Follow the signal. Read the object.

  1. Choose a world and travel to a mission region.
  2. Approach a beacon to scan its historical hardware.
  3. Read what it was, why it went, and what it found.
  4. Try a hands-on instrument challenge and track suit resources.
  5. Use the NASA archive to follow the facts back to their records.
  6. Finish the survey and leave a marker at Tranquility Base.

A SELF-CONTAINED WEB EXPERIENCE

Walk the sites.
Bring the story home.

Explore procedural terrain inspired by lunar maria, highlands, Martian plains and asteroid Eros. Scan 20 field objects, solve six simplified instrument challenges, manage oxygen and power, shelter from hazards, and read 18 NSSDCA records.

Progress is saved locally in your browser. The 3D settings are scale-and-play approximations; the artifact stories and source links point back to mission records.
